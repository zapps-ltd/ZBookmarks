Tips and Tricks

For the best usage scenarios and quickest results.

Where ZBookmarks saves your shortcuts

By default ZBookmarks saves your shortcuts in a folder named "Bookmarks and Shortcuts" on your Desktop, and creates it the first time it needs it.

You can choose another folder in Settings. The window shows the folder it saves to at the bottom, after "Saves to".

A dedicated folder keeps your new shortcuts apart from everything else, so your desktop does not get overcrowded. For example, choose your Favorites folder in Settings, and every shortcut you create is saved there without you going to the desktop.

Tip: put a ZBookmarks shortcut on your Desktop from Settings and give it a global hotkey, for example Control plus Shift plus F. Then you can open it from anywhere, create the shortcut, and go back to whatever you were doing.

More: you can move the shortcuts you use most from that folder to your desktop, and even assign a hotkey to open each one for convenience.

So, it serves well for:

  • Instead of typing frequently visited websites or pages, copy the page URL once, open ZBookmarks, and you will find it already pasted if configured to do so, as well as the webpage name already fetched and pasted in the Name field. Just hit Enter on the Name field — that should create the favorite or shortcut file for you wherever you placed the ZBookmarks exe file. Or if you prefer tabbing around, you will find all the buttons properly labeled — activate them with the Spacebar key to do the tasks.
  • Instead of fighting your computer digging for folders, or using Windows Start search and using arrow keys to select whatever folder or file result you are trying to locate again — find the folder or file, select it, press and hold the Shift key and press F10 or the Applications key while holding it. Arrow down or up until you find Copy as path. Paste it into the ZBookmarks first edit field. Press Tab, type a name for the shortcut, and hit Enter. Done. It works the same way as website favorites, except it does not fetch the names and paste the info automatically.

Final tip: Assign a hotkey to any desktop shortcut

Press Alt plus Enter on the targeted shortcut, or press the Applications key then arrow up to Properties and hit Enter.

Either right away or tab to find, and listen for: Shortcut key, edit, None.

Type the hotkey combination you want, then hit Enter, or Tab to the OK button and press Enter or Spacebar on it.

After that, pressing that hotkey will open or activate whatever the related shortcut was created for — a webpage, file, folder, or a program.
